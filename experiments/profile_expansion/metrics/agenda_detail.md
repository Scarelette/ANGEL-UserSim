Details on each topic
1. Presenting Problem / Chief Complaint
Goal:
Understand why the patient is seeking help now, how they describe the main concern in their own words, and what they hope to receive from the interview or treatment.
Core interview questions:
What brings you in today?
What are you hoping we can help you with?
How would you describe the problem?
What feels most important for me to understand about what you are going through?
Information to extract:
Chief complaint
Patient’s own words for the problem
Main concern
Problem framing
Reason for seeking help
Current urgency
Expectations for help

2. Major Symptoms
Goal:
Identify the patient’s major symptoms, especially what troubles them most. Symptoms should be organized into emotions, behaviors, and cognitions.
2a. Emotions
Goal:
Understand the patient’s emotional experience.
Core interview questions:
What emotions have you been feeling most often?
What feels most distressing emotionally?
How intense do these feelings get?
How often do these emotions come up?
Information to extract:
Dominant emotion
Secondary emotions
Emotional intensity
Emotional frequency
Emotional duration
Mood changes
Anxiety or fear
Anger or irritability
Sadness or hopelessness
Shame or guilt
2b. Behaviors
Goal:
Understand how the problem shows up in the patient’s behavior.
Core interview questions:
What do you do when the problem gets worse?
Have you changed how you act or what you do day to day?
Are there things you avoid because of the problem?
Have other people noticed changes in your behavior?
Information to extract:
Avoidance behavior
Withdrawal
Compulsions or repetitive behaviors
Changes in activity
Sleep behavior changes
Eating behavior changes
Agitation or restlessness
Functional behavioral changes
Maladaptive behavior patterns
2c. Cognitions
Goal:
Understand the patient’s thoughts, beliefs, worries, interpretations, and cognitive patterns.
Core interview questions:
What thoughts usually come up when you feel distressed?
What do you worry about most?
What do you tell yourself about the situation?
How do you interpret what is happening to you?
Information to extract:
Negative thoughts
Worries
Rumination
Self-critical beliefs
Beliefs about others
Beliefs about the future
Interpretations of events
Thought constriction
Obsessional thoughts
Hopeless thoughts

3. History of Problem
Goal:
Understand when the problem began, how it developed over time, whether it has happened before, and what events or contexts may have precipitated or worsened it.
3a. Onset / Timeline
Goal:
Establish the chronology and course of the presenting problem.
Core interview questions:
How long has this been a concern?
When did this first begin?
Is this the first time you have had these problems?
If not, how many times has this happened before?
When was the first time?
Has the problem changed over time?
Information to extract:
Onset time
Duration
First episode or recurrent problem
Number of episodes
Course over time
Changes in severity
Periods of improvement
Periods of worsening
Past similar problems
3b. Triggers / Precipitating Events
Goal:
Identify events, stressors, or situations that started, reactivated, or worsened the problem.
Core interview questions:
According to you, what caused the problem?
Were there any new events happening in your life?
Was there anything stressful happening?
What events or situations make the problem worse?
Are there situations where the problem feels better?
Can you tell me more about how these events are linked to your problem?
Information to extract:
Perceived cause
Precipitating events
Recent stressors
Trigger events
Trigger contexts
Trigger frequency
Trigger specificity
Patient interpretation of triggers
Situations that worsen the problem
Situations that improve the problem

4. Impact on Functioning
Goal:
Assess how the problem affects the patient’s daily life, including work or school, relationships, health, self-care, and everyday tasks.
Core interview questions:
How has this problem affected your life?
Can you do your usual work or everyday tasks?
Have you noticed any changes in your relationships?
How has it affected your work, school, health, or daily responsibilities?
What does a typical day look like for you now?
Information to extract:
Work or school impact
Relationship impact
Everyday task impact
Self-care impact
Health impact
Social functioning impact
Role functioning impact
Typical day disruption
Functional impairment level

5. Current Coping
Goal:
Understand how the patient currently deals with the problem, what helps, what makes it worse, and whether avoidance or other maladaptive coping patterns are present.
Core interview questions:
How have you been coping or dealing with this?
What helps?
What makes it worse?
Are there times when the problem feels better?
Are there times when it feels worse?
What do you usually do when the distress becomes strong?
Information to extract:
Coping strategies
Helpful coping
Maladaptive coping
Avoidance behavior
Safety behaviors
Emotion regulation strategies
Problem-solving attempts
Coping effectiveness
Barriers to coping
Situations when better
Situations when worse

6. Social Support
Goal:
Assess who is important in the patient’s life, the quality of available support, whether the patient feels isolated, and what others have suggested.
Core interview questions:
Who are the important people in your life that you see or talk to regularly?
Do you have close friends or family?
Would you like to have more friends or support?
What do your family, friends, or other people suggest you do?
Who do you usually turn to when things are difficult?

Family support
Friend support
Community support

Desired support
Barriers to support
Others’ suggestions

7. Past Treatment / Psychiatric History
Goal:
Collect prior treatment experiences, psychiatric diagnoses, hospitalizations, medication history, previous episodes, family mental health history, and barriers to seeking help.
Core interview questions:
Have you ever received help for this or similar problems?
What kind of help did you receive, when, and for how long?
What was useful or not useful about it?
Have you ever received a formal mental health diagnosis?
Have you ever been hospitalized for psychological problems or stressors?
Have you had prior psychological or psychiatric treatment?
Has anything gotten in the way of getting help?
Are you aware of similar concerns among family members?
Information to extract:
Prior help-seeking
Therapy history
Medication history
Prior diagnoses
Hospitalization history
Outpatient treatment history
Significant psychiatric episodes
Treatment duration
Treatment response
Helpful treatment elements
Unhelpful treatment elements
Barriers to treatment
Family mental health history

8. Risk Assessment
Goal:
Assess safety concerns, including suicide or self-harm, harm to others, abuse or victimization, substance use, access to means, risk factors, and protective factors.
Suicide / Self-Harm
Core interview questions:
Have you had thoughts that life was not worth living?
Have you thought you would be better off dead?
Have you attempted to hurt yourself or end your life?
How often do these thoughts occur?
When was the last time you had these thoughts?
Have you made plans to end your life?
Have you ever thought about how you might go about it?
Have you come close to acting on these thoughts?
Do you have access to medications, guns, or other means?
What keeps you going?
Who do you go to when you feel this way?

Prior attempts

Have you ever had thoughts, made statements, or attempted to hurt others?
Have you recently been physically hurt or threatened by someone else?

9. Treatment Goal / Hope
Goal:
Understand the patient’s goals for treatment, expectations for therapy, desired changes, strengths, sources of resilience, and any remaining concerns.
Core interview questions:

What do you picture therapy to look like?
What are you hoping will change?
What do you think are your greatest strengths?
Is there anything else we may have missed or have not covered today?
Information to extract:
Treatment goals
Desired changes
Therapy expectations
Hopefulness
Motivation for treatment
Strengths
Resilience factors
Hobbies or activities
Spiritual or religious support
Additional concerns
